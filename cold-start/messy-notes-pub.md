# Case title
Meeting notes to action plan

## Scenario brief
A cross-functional team just finished a project update meeting. The notes are incomplete, a bit chaotic, and written in shorthand. The goal is to turn them into a clear action plan that can be shared with the team and leadership.

### Example messy notes
Launch still on track? maybe next Fri.

Priya to check vendor status.

Need final approval from ops.

Data validation issue in report 2.

Team said test results looked okay, but not all done.

Follow up with finance on budget release.

Someone to confirm if customer comms are needed.

Ask Dev to fix dashboard filters.

UAT pending, maybe 3 blockers.

Need to update everyone after this.
.

.

.

.

.

.

.

> “Turn these notes into action items.”

## Is it?

- Structured and easy to scan.
- Clear about owners, deadlines, and blockers.
- Useful for sharing after the meeting.
- Careful not to invent missing details.
- Appropriate for both team use and leadership updates.
- Did the AI create owners or deadlines that were not present?
- Did it separate action items from blockers correctly?
- Did it capture the likely meaning of shorthand notes?
- Was the output too verbose or too sparse?
- Did adding structure make the result significantly better?
.

.

.

.

.

.

.
## Likely improvement path

- Audience: project team and leadership.
- Tone: concise, professional, action-oriented.
- Goal: extract action items, risks, and follow-ups.
- Structure: table or bullet list with owner, task, status, deadline, and risk.
- Constraint: do not invent names, dates, or facts that are not in the notes.
.

.

.

.

## Stronger prompt version
> “Convert the following messy meeting notes into a clear action plan for the project team. Organize the output into sections for action items, blockers, decisions, and follow-ups. For each action item, include owner if mentioned, task, and any deadline or dependency if available. Keep the tone concise and professional. Do not invent missing details.”
.

.

.

.

.

.

.
## Even better prompt version
> “Read these messy project update notes and turn them into a structured action plan that can be shared with the team and leadership. Use the following format: 1) Summary, 2) Action items, 3) Blockers/Risks, 4) Pending decisions, 5) Follow-up communication. Extract only what is supported by the notes, and clearly mark anything uncertain as ‘not specified’. Keep it concise, clear, and ready to paste into a team update email or meeting follow-up document.”
