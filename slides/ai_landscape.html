AI Landscape Intro

History, categories, LLM training, agents

Simple framing

A useful rule of thumb

AI

A broad umbrella: systems that do tasks we associate with human intelligence, such as perception, prediction, language, and decision support.

ML

A subset of AI: instead of hand-writing every rule, the system learns patterns from data.

DL

A subset of ML: large neural networks learn richer patterns, especially from images, audio, and language.

GenAI

A newer family of AI that generates text, images, code, audio, and actions based on what it has learned.

The big journey

Rules era

Expert systems

If-then logic; works well when rules are stable, brittle when reality gets messy.

ML era

Learning from examples

Models learn from labeled or unlabeled data instead of only following explicit rules.

DL era

Neural networks scale

More data + more compute + deeper networks unlock major gains in speech, vision, and language.

GenAI era

Generate, not just classify

Models can draft content, summarize, reason over patterns, and simulate dialogue.

Agentic era

Plan and act

Models are increasingly wrapped with memory, tools, workflows, and evaluation loops.

Analogy slide

Three simple analogies

📘

Machine learning

Like showing a trainee many past cases so they learn patterns from examples.

🧠

Deep learning

Like giving that trainee many layers of pattern-recognition muscles so subtle signals get picked up.

✍️

Generative AI

Like asking the trainee not just to recognize patterns, but to create a first draft, answer, image, or plan.

Main learning styles

Supervised

Learn from labeled examples

Example: spam vs not-spam, loan default prediction, disease detection from known examples.

Unsupervised

Find structure without labels

Example: customer segmentation, anomaly discovery, topic grouping in documents.

Reinforcement

Learn by trial and feedback

Example: game-playing agents, robot control, dynamic optimization where actions have consequences.

Neural nets

Neural nets, without the math overload

Think layers

Input goes in, many hidden layers transform it, output comes out. Each layer learns more useful features than the one before.

Think weights

Training adjusts millions or billions of tiny weights so the network gets better at mapping input to useful output.

Why deep mattered

Enough data and compute let deeper networks beat older methods on speech, vision, translation, and later language generation.

Rule of thumb

Classical ML often needs hand-crafted features; deep learning often learns those features on its own.

LLMs

What an LLM is really trained to do

Core objective

Predict the next token well enough, over vast amounts of text, that language structure and world patterns emerge.

Then aligned

After base training, models are further tuned to follow instructions, be safer, and respond in more useful formats.

What users see

A chat interface, but underneath it is token prediction plus system prompts, tooling, ranking, and guardrails.

How LLM training works

What they train on

Large text corpora: books, websites, articles, code, docs, conversations, and increasingly multimodal data depending on the model.

Where training runs

Huge GPU/accelerator clusters in hyperscale data centers, often spread across racks and regions.

How long it takes

Typically weeks to months when pretraining very large frontier models; smaller domain models can be far faster.

What changes quality

Data quality, diversity, recency, cleaning, model architecture, compute budget, and fine-tuning choices all matter.

Why training data quality matters

✅

Clean and diverse

Better generalization, broader coverage, and fewer brittle blind spots.

⚠️

Biased or noisy

More errors, uneven behavior across tasks, and more risk of harmful or low-trust outputs.

🕒

Outdated

Strong older knowledge, weak recent facts; this is one reason retrieval and tool use matter today.

Now

The shift to agentic systems

From answer engines

Early systems mostly responded to prompts with one answer at a time.

To workflow engines

Newer systems can plan steps, use tools, fetch data, call APIs, route tasks, and ask follow-up questions.

Human role

The human moves from “writer of every output” to supervisor, reviewer, and process designer.

Operational need

Evaluation, guardrails, monitoring, and human approval become even more important.

Future-facing

What to expect next

More multimodal

Text, image, audio, video, and interface actions blend into one experience.

More embedded

AI moves into everyday tools, business processes, and operating models rather than staying as a separate app.

More responsibility

Trust, policy, governance, copyright, privacy, and evaluation become core business capabilities.
