What it actually looks like in your job.

Overview

The same underlying model can do very different things depending on what you give it.

Type of task

Writing, analysis, code, research, decision support

Risk profile

A wrong answer in a legal memo has different consequences than in a brainstorm

Inputs you work with

Structured data vs. unstructured documents vs. code

Output format needed

A paragraph, a table, a script, a slide

Business & Strategy

Where AI compresses thinking time

Use CaseWhat to ask AI to do
Executive briefingSummarize a long report into a 5-bullet brief with key decisions highlighted
Competitor scanGiven 3 competitor press releases, synthesize positioning differences and flag strategic gaps
Workshop facilitationGenerate a structured agenda, discussion questions, and a pre-read summary from a brief
Stakeholder communicationDraft a "what this means for you" message tailored to a specific audience (board, client, frontline)

One thing to watch out for

AI will give you confident-sounding strategic analysis — but it doesn't know your client, market context, or internal politics. Use it to structure thinking, not replace it.

Business & Strategy — worked example

From a 40-page report to a 5-minute read

Task: Executive summary of a market entry report for a leadership review.

System: "You are a strategy consultant preparing a briefing for a C-suite audience."

Below is a market entry analysis report. Extract:

1. The 3 most important findings

2. The key recommendation and its rationale

3. The top 2 risks and proposed mitigations

4. One open question the leadership team needs to resolve

Format as a one-page briefing. Use plain, direct language — no jargon.

[PASTE REPORT TEXT]

Output excerpt

"Key Finding 1: The target segment is growing at 18% YoY, but 60% of that growth is concentrated in two metropolitan corridors — expansion outside those markets carries significantly higher execution risk.

Recommendation: Enter via a partnership model in Year 1 rather than organic build, to reduce time-to-market and capital exposure..."

Time saved

~2 hours of summarising → ~10 minutes of prompting and editing.

Finance & Accounting

From numbers to narrative, faster

Use CaseWhat to ask AI to do
Variance commentaryPaste actuals vs. budget table; ask for a 3-paragraph CFO commentary draft with key drivers
Audit prepSummarize a standard (e.g., IFRS 16) in plain language; generate a checklist of disclosure requirements
Financial model documentationDescribe assumptions embedded in a model; AI drafts the methodology section
Client reportingConvert a raw data table into a narrative paragraph for a client report

One thing to watch out for

Numbers don't go into public AI tools unless your firm has an enterprise agreement with data isolation. Paste anonymized or dummy data for testing; use the enterprise-approved tool for real figures.

Finance — worked example

Variance analysis in plain English

Task: Draft management commentary for a monthly P&L review.

System: "You are a senior finance analyst."

Here is the P&L variance table for Q3: [PASTE TABLE]

Write 3 paragraphs of management commentary:

- Para 1: Overall performance vs. budget

- Para 2: Key drivers of variance (positive and negative)

- Para 3: Outlook and items requiring leadership attention

Use precise financial language. Flag line items where variance exceeds 10%.

Output excerpt

"Q3 revenue came in 4.2% below budget, primarily driven by a shortfall in the APAC region (−8.1%) which offset a modest outperformance in EMEA (+2.4%). Gross margin held steady at 38.6%, within 0.3pp of plan, as cost discipline offset the revenue miss..."

Time saved

First draft of management commentary in under 5 minutes vs. 45–60 min from scratch.

HR & Talent

Consistency and speed across the employee lifecycle

Use CaseWhat to ask AI to do
Job descriptionsDraft a JD from a role brief; calibrate language to seniority level and inclusion standards
Interview scorecardsFrom a JD, generate behavioral competencies + interview questions + scoring rubric
Policy draftingDraft a first version of an HR policy (e.g., flexible working, AI use) from a set of principles

One thing to watch out for

AI-generated JDs and policies can carry inherited bias (gendered language, credential inflation). Always review with an inclusion lens before publishing.

HR — worked example

Interview scorecard from a job description

Task: Build a structured interview guide for a Senior Financial Analyst role.

System: "You are an experienced HR business partner."

Based on the JD below, create a structured interview scorecard.

Include:

- 5 behavioral competencies relevant to this role

- 2 interview questions per competency

- A 1–4 scoring rubric (what "1" and "4" look like)

- One technical question per competency where relevant

[PASTE JD]

Output excerpt

Competency: Analytical Rigour

Q1: Tell me about a time you found an error in a financial model that others had missed. How did you catch it?

Score 1: Describes finding an error by chance, no systematic approach

Score 4: Describes a repeatable review process; caught the error before it reached stakeholders; quantified impact

Legal, Risk & Compliance

Research and drafting speed with human sign-off

Use CaseWhat to ask AI to do
Contract first-readSummarize key obligations, rights, termination clauses, and red flags in a contract draft
Regulatory researchExplain a regulation in plain language; identify which sections apply to a specific scenario
Policy gap analysisCompare two policy documents; flag differences and identify gaps against a standard
Risk register draftingFrom a project description, generate a first-pass risk register with likelihood/impact ratings

One thing to watch out for

AI is not a lawyer and does not have access to current case law or jurisdiction-specific updates. Use it for first-pass research and drafting — every substantive output needs legal review before use.

Legal — worked example

Contract summary in minutes, not hours

Task: First-read summary of a vendor services agreement.

System: "You are a commercial lawyer conducting a first-read review."

Summarize the following contract and flag:

1. Key obligations on each party

2. Payment terms and any unusual fee structures

3. Termination rights (for cause and for convenience)

4. Liability caps and exclusions

5. Any clauses that are unusual, one-sided, or worth negotiating

Use a table format. Flag high-risk items in bold.

[PASTE CONTRACT TEXT]

Output excerpt

ClauseSummaryRisk
Payment (8.2)Net-60; late interest 12% p.a.⚠️ Unusual — standard is Net-30
Liability cap (14)Capped at 1× fees paid in prior 3 months⚠️ Low cap — consider 12 months
Termination (19)Vendor can terminate with 7 days' notice⚠️ Asymmetric — client requires 90 days

Technology & Engineering

AI as a tireless coding partner

Use CaseWhat to ask AI to do
Code generationWrite a function from a plain-English description; generate boilerplate, tests, or data fixtures
Code reviewExplain what a code block does; identify potential bugs, edge cases, or security issues
DocumentationGenerate docstrings, README sections, or API documentation from code
DebuggingPaste an error + stack trace; get a diagnosis and suggested fix
Architecture discussionDescribe a design problem; get a structured trade-off analysis of 2–3 approaches

One thing to watch out for

AI-generated code is plausible, not guaranteed correct. It confidently writes code with subtle bugs, outdated APIs, or security holes. Always review and test — treat it like output from a fast junior developer.

Engineering — worked example

From stack trace to root cause in 2 minutes

Task: Debug a production error in a Python data pipeline.

I'm getting this error in a Python ETL pipeline.

Here's the traceback and the relevant function:

[PASTE ERROR + CODE]

1. Explain what is causing this error in plain language

2. Identify the exact line where it originates

3. Suggest a fix with a code snippet

4. Are there related edge cases I should handle at the same time?

Output excerpt

"The error is a KeyError on line 47, occurring because the function assumes the key 'transaction_date' always exists, but records from the legacy system omit it when the date is null."

Fix: Replace record['transaction_date'] with record.get('transaction_date', None)

Edge cases: (1) Date exists but is empty string ""; (2) Legacy format DD/MM/YYYY vs. new system ISO format.

Data & Analytics

From query to insight, faster

Use CaseWhat to ask AI to do
SQL generationDescribe the query in plain English; AI writes the SQL; you review and run it
Insight narrationPaste a table of results; ask for a 2-paragraph insight summary for a non-technical audience
Dashboard copyWrite chart titles, axis labels, and tooltips that communicate meaning, not just data
Exploratory analysis planDescribe a dataset and business question; AI proposes an analysis approach and metrics to track

One thing to watch out for

AI-generated SQL can be syntactically correct but logically wrong (wrong join type, missing filter, incorrect aggregation). Always validate query results against a known small dataset before running at scale.

Data — worked example

Turning a result set into a narrative

Task: Write an insight paragraph from a churn analysis for a business review.

System: "You are a data analyst presenting to a non-technical business audience."

Here are the results of a customer churn analysis:

[PASTE TABLE: churn rate by segment, tenure, product type]

Write 2 paragraphs:

- Para 1: What the data shows (key patterns, highest-risk segments)

- Para 2: What this means for the business and what action it suggests

Avoid technical jargon. Write for a VP of Sales who reads fast.

Output excerpt

"Churn is heavily concentrated among customers in their first 90 days, particularly in the SMB segment using the entry-level product tier — this group churns at 3.4x the rate of mid-market customers on the standard plan. Customers who complete onboarding in the first two weeks show dramatically better retention regardless of segment.

The data points to a clear early-intervention opportunity: if the onboarding gap can be closed for SMB customers, the model suggests a 12–15% reduction in overall churn is achievable without acquiring a single new customer..."
