AI Strategy & What's Next

Making smart decisions about AI adoption — and where this is all going.

The three approaches

There are only three ways to make an AI model more useful

Most enterprise AI projects fail not because the technology is wrong, but because teams pick the wrong approach for the problem.

ApproachWhat it isComplexityCost
PromptingBetter instructions, no data changesLowNear-zero
RAGConnect model to your knowledge at query timeMediumLow–Medium
Fine-tuningTrain the model further on your dataHighHigh

The right order of attempt: prompting first, RAG if needed, fine-tuning rarely.

Prompting

Most problems are solved before you touch the data layer

When prompting alone is enough

  • • You need a general-purpose assistant for drafting, summarizing, or reasoning
  • • The information needed is already in the conversation (you paste in the document)
  • • You want consistent tone, format, or behavior → use a well-designed system prompt
  • • Your use case is exploratory or low-volume

Limitations

  • • Doesn't help if the model lacks knowledge you need (internal policies, proprietary data)
  • • Inconsistency at scale: humans prompt differently without structure
  • • Context window is finite — you can't paste in everything

Before building anything, spend a week prompting. You'll be surprised how far you get.

RAG

Your knowledge, the model's reasoning

At query time, retrieve relevant documents from your knowledge base → feed them into the prompt → model reasons over your content, not its training data.

When RAG is the right call

  • • You have proprietary knowledge the model doesn't know (policies, contracts, product docs)
  • • You need answers to be grounded in specific, verifiable sources
  • • Your knowledge changes frequently (model training is a snapshot; RAG is live)
  • • You need to cite sources in the output

Limitations

  • • Only as good as your retrieval — garbage in, garbage out
  • • Doesn't change how the model writes or reasons, only what it knows
  • • Needs infrastructure: a vector database, chunking pipeline, embedding step

RAG solves the "the model doesn't know our stuff" problem. Fine-tuning doesn't.

Fine-tuning

Powerful but rarely the first move

Additional training on your labeled dataset shifts the model's style, tone, or domain knowledge at a deeper level than prompting can achieve.

When it's genuinely worth it

  • • You need a very specific writing style, consistently, at scale
  • • You're building a specialized domain assistant with thousands of examples
  • • You want to reduce prompt size/cost in a high-volume production system

Why it's rarely the first move

Requires labeled data (expensive), training compute (costly), re-training on model updates. A well-designed RAG + system prompt usually gets 80% of the way there first.

Decision matrix

ProblemTry first
"The model doesn't know our policies"RAG
"The model writes in the wrong style"System prompt → then fine-tune if still wrong
"The model gets our domain wrong"Few-shot → RAG → then fine-tune
"We need low latency at massive scale"Fine-tune a smaller model

Multimodal AI

Beyond text

A single model that can accept and/or produce multiple types of content — not separate specialized tools, but one model that understands context across modalities.

InputWhat AI can do
Image / photoDescribe, analyze, extract text, identify objects, compare
Chart / graphRead values, summarize trends, explain patterns
Document (PDF, screenshot)Extract structured data, summarize, answer questions
Audio / recordingTranscribe, translate, summarize meeting notes
VoiceConverse in real time (GPT-4o, Gemini Live)

For your roles specifically

  • • Finance: paste a screenshot of a chart → ask for a narrative summary
  • • Legal: upload a scanned contract → ask for key clause extraction
  • • HR: transcribe an interview recording → generate a structured debrief
  • • Ops: photograph a whiteboard → convert to structured action items

Optional depth

The modality frontier is moving fast

What's arriving or maturing

  • • Video understanding — analyze recorded meetings, training videos, product demos (Gemini 1.5, GPT-4o Vision)
  • • Real-time voice — low-latency spoken conversation with full reasoning (GPT-4o Realtime, Gemini Live)
  • • Computer use — AI agents that can operate a browser or desktop on your behalf (Claude, Operator)
  • • Rich document processing — tables, forms, invoices, handwriting at production quality

The practical signal

If a task involves looking at something, listening to something, or navigating a UI — it's now on the table for AI assistance. The text-only mental model is already outdated.

Multimodal isn't a feature — it's the new baseline for frontier models.

The question to ask

Are we using AI to do the same things faster, or to do things we couldn't do before?

Build / Buy / Integrate

You rarely need to build from scratch — but you do need to make a choice

Buy

Use a product as-is

ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini Workspace, etc. Fast, zero infrastructure, good for individual productivity.

Limited customization, data goes to provider.

Integrate

API + your own logic

Connect a model to your systems via API; build your own UX or automation. Flexible, can use your data, production-grade.

Requires engineering; you manage reliability and security.

Build

Train or fine-tune your own

Maximum control; no external dependency.

Expensive, slow, requires ML expertise. Justified only at significant scale or in highly regulated environments.

The default for most teams: Buy for individual use, Integrate for workflows, Build only when forced to.

Common mistakes

The mistakes organizations make are predictable — and avoidable

Piloting forever

Running experiments that never become production. A pilot that hasn't shipped after 3 months is probably a culture problem, not a technology problem.

Ignoring change management

Rolling out an AI tool without training, guidance, or a policy. Technology adoption is 20% tool, 80% behavior change.

Over-indexing on the tool

Choosing a model before defining the use case. Start with: What specific task takes the most time with the least value? Can AI help with that?

No human-in-the-loop

Automating decisions about people, money, or legal matters without review. AI augments judgment; it doesn't replace it for consequential decisions.

The organizations winning with AI right now aren't the ones with the best models — they're the ones with the clearest use cases and the best habits around using AI outputs.

What's coming

The trajectory is clear even when the timeline isn't

TrendWhat it means practically
Longer context windowsFeed entire codebases, document archives, call transcripts into one prompt
Better reasoningComplex multi-step analysis with fewer errors; less need for chain-of-thought scaffolding
Cheaper inferenceRunning AI on tasks that were cost-prohibitive before
Agent frameworks maturingAI that completes multi-step tasks autonomously with minimal supervision
Smaller, faster modelsAI embedded in devices, offline, real-time

What this means for you

  • • The gap between "AI-assisted" and "AI-augmented" work will close fast
  • • Routine information work will be almost entirely AI-assisted within 2–3 years
  • • The bottleneck shifts from "can AI do this?" to "do we have the process to use it well?"

Human edge

The most valuable thing you can do is ask the right question

As AI handles more of the execution layer, what becomes scarcer — and more valuable:

Judgment

Knowing when an AI output is wrong, incomplete, or dangerous to use

Framing

Translating a messy real-world problem into a precise AI task

Evaluation

Assessing outputs against standards the AI can't fully internalize

Context

Understanding the human, organizational, and ethical dimensions of a decision

"The question isn't 'will AI take my job?' — it's 'am I developing the judgment layer that AI can't replicate?'"

The skill gap of the next decade isn't coding. It's critical evaluation of AI output.

Closing

The foundation is in place — what happens next is up to you

1. Pick one real task

Something you do weekly that's tedious. Spend 30 minutes this week prompting AI through it. See what's possible.

2. Share one finding

Tell a colleague what worked, what didn't, what surprised you. AI adoption spreads through networks, not mandates.

3. Stay sceptical and curious

Verify outputs for anything consequential. The people who use AI well are the ones who never fully trust it blindly.
