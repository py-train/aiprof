# Sample Workshop Notes — Client Onboarding Improvement
**For use with:** `day1-coldstart-exercise.md`, Part 1 Prompt 1b  
**What these are:** Realistic rough notes from a fictional two-day internal workshop. Intentionally messy — abbreviations, incomplete sentences, some contradictions, no polish. This is what real working notes look like.

---

## DAY 1 NOTES

**Attendees:** Priya (workshop lead), James (Client Services), Nadia (Operations), Tom (Tech), 2× regional account managers (Liz + David)

**Morning — Current state review**

- James opened: "onboarding is taking 6–8 weeks on average, client feedback says they feel abandoned after contract sign"
- Mapped current process on whiteboard — 14 steps from contract to go-live
- Big finding: 3 handoffs with no SLA — contract → CS, CS → Ops, Ops → Tech
- Nadia flagged: Ops team often gets incomplete info from CS at handoff — has to go back to client
- Tom: "we don't even have a shared system of record — everyone working from email threads"
- Voted: top 3 pain points = (1) info gaps at handoffs, (2) no client visibility into status, (3) welcome comms feel generic/not personalised

**Afternoon — Benchmarking + ideas**

- Liz presented quick benchmarks: competitors averaging 3–4 wk onboarding
- Reviewed 2 case studies from industry (fintech, professional services)
- Ideas generated (no filtering yet): client portal, dedicated onboarding manager role, standardised intake form, automated status emails, "welcome kit" redesign, kickoff call template
- Decision: agree to focus only on handoff + client comms problems for this sprint — don't boil the ocean
- Tom: portal is 6–12 months away (resource constrained). Not in scope for now.

---

## DAY 2 NOTES

**Morning — Solutions deep-dive**

- Nadia walked through proposed new intake form — 12 fields, captures everything Ops needs
- Debate: James thinks 12 fields is too many for clients to fill in. Nadia: "we need all of it." Unresolved — agreed to pilot and see drop-off rate
- David (account mgr): "clients want to know someone is responsible" — strong support for named onboarding contact per client
- Agreed: each new client gets assigned onboarding lead from CS. Not a new hire — redistribute existing workload for now.
- Draft SLA proposed: CS → Ops handoff within 48hrs of contract. Ops → Tech within 5 days. Tom pushed back on 5 days ("depends on complexity") — compromise: 5 days standard, escalation path for complex clients TBD

**Afternoon — Implementation planning**

- Broke into 3 workstreams:
  1. **Intake form** — Nadia owns, pilot with 3 clients in Q3, review after
  2. **Onboarding lead assignment** — James owns, comms to team by end of month
  3. **Client status emails** — Tom to spec out automated triggers, timeline unclear ("need to check with engineering")
- Welcome kit redesign: everyone agreed it's needed but no owner. Priya: "parking lot for now"
- KPIs agreed: time-to-go-live (target: under 4 weeks by end of year), client satisfaction at 30-day mark (NPS question to be added)
- Risks noted: CS capacity (currently stretched), tech resource dependency for status emails
- Priya: "nothing here is revolutionary but if we just execute these basics we'll be ahead of most competitors"

**Loose ends / open questions**
- Intake form field count still debated
- Tom's "5 days" SLA not fully signed off
- Welcome kit redesign needs an owner
- Status email automation depends on engineering capacity check (Tom to follow up by Friday)

## How to use these notes in the cold-start exercise

See `day1-coldstart-exercise.md` → **Part 1, Prompt 1b**

Participants copy these notes into their second attempt (after running Prompt 1 with no context). The contrast they should notice:

| | Prompt 1 (no notes) | Prompt 1b (with notes) |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Fabricated but plausible | Based on actual workshop content |
| Specificity | Generic topics, placeholder names | Real pain points, real decisions, real owners |
| Reliability | Entirely invented | Mostly accurate to the notes — but watch for synthesis errors |
| What AI does | Generates form without substance | Synthesizes and organises real content |

**Discussion point to draw out in debrief:** The output is now much more useful — but is it *trustworthy*? What would you still verify before sending?
